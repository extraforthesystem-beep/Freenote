# Build the notebook that never loses notes

The opening for a new handwriting app in late 2026 is reliability and ownership, not more features. Goodnotes and Notability already cover every standard tool, yet a majority of their recent written reviews are one or two stars, driven by lost notes, paywalls that lock existing notebooks, bugs in core editing, and AI nobody asked for. Your three stated requirements map straight onto that gap: an Apple Pencil-first iPad app, a Mac companion, and sync through your own Google Drive, which no competitor offers as true two-way sync. Apple's 2026 frameworks make the iPad side far cheaper than it was: palm rejection, eight pen styles, Pencil gestures and on-device handwriting search now come free with the system. The recommended plan is a paged notebook app in SwiftUI on Apple's ink framework, storing each notebook as plain per-page files, shipped in three phases: a local iPad app first, then Drive sync and the Mac app, then optional extras such as audio replay, layers and AI. Two-way Drive sync is both your differentiator and the largest engineering risk, because Drive offers no safe-overwrite check and the app must handle conflicts itself. Seven decisions are yours to make before building starts, and they are listed in the last section.

## Incumbents lose users to lost notes and locked notebooks, not missing tools

Both market leaders carry near-perfect star averages (Goodnotes 4.72, Notability 4.76), but the written reviews tell a different story. In the 50 most recent US reviews of each app, **22 of Goodnotes' and 33 of Notability's were one or two stars** ([Goodnotes review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1444383602/sortby=mostrecent/json), [Notability review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=360593530/sortby=mostrecent/json)). A study by a competing notes app found the same pattern over six months, with 54% and 64% low-star written reviews respectively; treat that figure as directional because of who published it ([Harbor](https://harbor.my/blog/notes-app-complaints/)).

The complaints cluster into four themes, and none of them is a missing feature. The first is data loss. Notability reviewers describe notes "wiped completely clean" after its move to its own cloud, with one writing that "nearly 10 years of work is just gone" ([Notability review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=360593530/sortby=mostrecent/json)). The second is paywalls that reach back into existing work: Goodnotes makes notebooks above the free limit read-only after a downgrade ([Goodnotes plans](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/13808767840015-Compare-Goodnotes-Plans-and-Features)), which is what people who bought the app years ago are angry about. The third is broken core editing. A Goodnotes bug that duplicates handwriting when it is moved with the lasso was reported in February 2025 and again in a November 2026 review ([r/GoodNotes](https://www.reddit.com/r/GoodNotes/comments/1iiiod8/how_did_goodnotes_become_such_a_nightmare_full_of/), [Goodnotes review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1444383602/sortby=mostrecent/json)). The fourth is unwanted AI, which appears in roughly 9% of angry Goodnotes reviews and 13% of Notability's ([Harbor](https://harbor.my/blog/notes-app-complaints/)).

What users praise is a short, stable list: a pen that feels immediate, palm rejection they can trust, a lasso that moves ink without damaging it, shape snapping, PDF annotation, handwriting search, and fast navigation through tabs, bookmarks and a sidebar. Handwriting search is the only "smart" feature that shows up in both praise and switching decisions. The most-requested missing features on Goodnotes' own request board are structural: a one-tap bookmark, an optional toolbar, notes stored as visible files, and better pen feel ([Goodnotes feedback board](https://feedback.goodnotes.com/forums/191274-customer-suggestions-for-goodnotes-apple/suggestions/50653646-icloud-drive-sync)). Smaller apps such as Noteful win switchers with a one-time price plus specific fixes like layers and five pen sizes ([r/GoodNotes](https://www.reddit.com/r/GoodNotes/comments/1pockts/im_sad_to_say_this_but_switching_to_noteful_was/)).

On features, the two leaders have converged. Both now organise with nested folders, after Notability replaced its subjects and dividers in March 2026 ([Notability update history](https://support.gingerlabs.com/en-us/articles/16300220-app-update-history)). They differ in document types: Goodnotes offers paged notebooks, an infinite whiteboard and typed documents, while Notability has one paged note with a continuous-scroll view ([Goodnotes](https://www.goodnotes.com/), [Notability view settings](https://support.gingerlabs.com/en-us/articles/16300185-note-view-settings)). Only three capabilities are rare enough to count as differentiators: audio recordings linked to handwriting so that tapping a word replays that moment (Notability), drawing layers (Goodnotes, early access on its top plan since mid-2026), and handwriting editing that keeps your own hand ([Notability audio](https://support.gingerlabs.com/en-us/articles/16300122-recording-and-playing-audio), [Goodnotes Q3 2026 release](https://www.goodnotes.com/blog/whats-new-in-goodnotes-july-september-2026)). Pricing runs from $11.99 to $35.99 a year at Goodnotes plus $9.99 a month for extra AI, and up to $79.99 a year at Notability ([Goodnotes pricing](https://www.goodnotes.com/pricing), [Notability pricing](https://notability.com/pricing)).

## Apple's frameworks now supply the pen, palm rejection and handwriting search

Your three additions (Apple Pencil, palm rejection, different pen styles) are all covered by PencilKit, Apple's drawing framework, without custom work. Its canvas has a pencil-only mode in which the Pencil draws and fingers only scroll ([drawing policy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pencilkit/pkcanvasviewdrawingpolicy)). It ships **eight pen styles**: pen, fountain pen, monoline, pencil, marker, crayon, watercolor and reed ([ink types](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pencilkit/pkinkingtool-swift.struct/inktype-swift.enum)). Pencil Pro squeeze, barrel roll, hover and haptics come largely free when the app adopts Apple's tool picker ([WWDC24 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10214/)).

The ceiling is control. PencilKit does not allow custom brush textures, your own pressure curves or effect erasers ([Customizing PencilKit](https://medium.com/@wesleymatlock/customizing-pencilkit-going-past-apples-tool-picker-b82eca7bfbe2)), and it has no layers. Goodnotes built its own rendering engine to get past that, and reports about 1.47 million lines of shared engine and logic code ([Swift.org](https://www.swift.org/blog/bringing-goodnotes-to-web-with-swift/)). For a solo build, start on PencilKit and treat a custom engine as a later decision, taken only if pen feel becomes the reason to choose the app.

Three formerly hard problems are now system features. **Handwriting recognition and search arrived in iPadOS 27 and macOS 27** as a free, on-device API that returns search hits with their position on the page ([PKStrokeRecognizer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pencilkit/pkstrokerecognizer)), which removes the need for a paid recognition library. The same release gives every stroke a stable identity, which is what makes stroke-level features (audio replay, merging edits from two devices) practical on top of Apple's canvas ([WWDC26 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/203/)). On-device speech transcription has been a public API since iPadOS 26 ([SpeechAnalyzer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechanalyzer)). Apple's PDF framework supports drawing over pages, burning annotations into an exported file, and following links, which hyperlinked planners depend on ([WWDC22 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10089/), [PDFViewDelegate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pdfkit/pdfviewdelegate)). One caution of my own, untested: a drawing layer on top of a PDF page will intercept taps, so link-following likely needs a read mode or tap pass-through.

SwiftUI is the right shell, with one structural rule: SwiftUI hosts the screen, and the drawing surface stays a wrapped system view that owns the live ink ([WWDC25 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/285/)). The Mac needs a deliberate choice, because **Apple's drawing canvas does not exist on native macOS**; it is listed for iPad and for Mac Catalyst, the mode that runs an iPad app on a Mac ([PKCanvasView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pencilkit/pkcanvasview)). Apple's newer markup framework, PaperKit, does run natively on both ([PaperKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/paperkit)). User evidence says Mac use is mostly reading and finding notes, then typing, then light editing, and that incumbents fail there on lag and sync, not on missing tools. That supports a native Mac app for reading, search, organising, typing and light annotation.

## Two-way Drive sync is the feature nobody ships, and the hardest part

Every incumbent uses Google Drive as a one-way backup. Goodnotes says so directly: "Auto Backup is a one-way process. It does not sync changes across your devices" ([Goodnotes Auto Backup](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/7352786555279-How-to-Set-Up-Auto-Backup-in-Goodnotes)). Notability's wording is the same ([Notability backup](https://support.gingerlabs.com/en-us/articles/16300164-backing-up-notes-outside-of-notability-cloud)). Real two-way sync through the user's own Drive would be a first in this category. It also means inheriting the problems those teams chose to avoid.

The permissions side is simple. The narrow `drive.file` permission lets the app read and write only the files it creates, is classed non-sensitive, and avoids Google's paid annual security audit ([Drive scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/api-specific-auth)). The Google project must not stay in "Testing" status, because there **sign-ins and refresh tokens expire after seven days** and sync would silently stop each week ([Google Cloud help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)). Usage quotas are far above anything a notes app needs ([Drive limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits)).

The hard part is correctness. Drive's file-update call has no documented way to say "save only if nobody else changed this file" ([files.update](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/reference/rest/v3/files/update)), and change notifications need a public web server, so a server-less app must poll for changes when it opens ([Drive push](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/push)). The design that fits these limits follows the pattern Joplin documents for syncing over simple file storage ([Joplin sync spec](https://joplinapp.org/help/dev/spec/sync/)): the device's local copy is the source of truth; each page is its own file, so a conflict affects one page at most; imported PDFs and audio are uploaded once and never re-sent; deletions are recorded as marker files so an offline device cannot resurrect them; and new files use pre-generated IDs so an interrupted upload can be retried safely ([Drive uploads](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/manage-uploads)). On iPad, background uploads must come from files on disk and the system throttles apps that start many small transfers, so changes should be batched ([Apple background transfers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/downloading-files-in-the-background)).

Conflict handling should come in two steps. First, when the same page was edited on both devices, keep both versions and show the conflict, which never loses work. Later, merge at the level of individual strokes, treating a page as a set of strokes plus a set of deleted-stroke markers. That second step is a design proposed in the research with no shipped example found, so it needs a prototype before it is promised. The research also suggested using iCloud for live sync and Drive only for backup; that comparison was not verified, and it contradicts your stated requirement, so it appears below as a decision, not a recommendation.

## Three phases, with the file format fixed first

The one thing that cannot be retrofitted is the storage format. Audio replay, layers and stroke merging all depend on what is recorded per stroke, so the format should carry a stable ID, a timestamp and a layer field for every stroke from the first version, plus a schema version number. Each notebook is a folder holding a small index file, one ink file per page, and an assets folder for PDFs, images and audio. That same layout is what Drive sync needs.

| Phase | Scope | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | iPad app, local only | Library with nested folders, favourites and tags; notebooks with pages, covers and per-page paper templates; eight pen styles with colours, widths and saved presets; highlighter; stroke and area eraser; lasso to move, resize and copy across pages; shape snapping; pencil-only palm rejection; Pencil double-tap, squeeze and hover; undo and redo; images and text boxes; PDF import and annotation with working links; page thumbnails, bookmarks and outline; tabs and a quick switcher; handwriting search across all notebooks; export to PDF and image; documented, editable file format |
| 2 | Sync and Mac | Google sign-in; Drive sync with keep-both conflict copies; visible sync status and restore; native Mac app for reading, search, organising, typing and light annotation; split view of two notes |
| 3 | Optional extras | Audio recording with tap-to-replay and on-device transcript; layers; stroke-level merge; infinite canvas as a second document type; page-scoped on-device AI; math conversion; presentation mode |

Phase 1 is the full table-stakes list from the competitor survey plus the navigation and search features users call essential. Audio replay sits in phase 3 only because you have not asked for it; technically it is small, since Notability describes the mechanism as a timestamp on every stroke and a filter at playback ([Notability engineering blog](https://blog.notability.com/post/how-we-built-live-transcription)). On-device AI is limited to page-sized tasks, because the research reports an on-device context of about 8,192 tokens ([WWDC26 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/)). Math recognition has no free system API and would need a commercial library whose price is not public ([MyScript pricing](https://developer.myscript.com/pricing/)).

| Decision | Options | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Personal use only, or App Store release | Decide first; a public release adds Google verification, a privacy policy and pricing |
| Pen engine | Apple's eight pens, or a custom engine with custom brushes | Apple's pens for phases 1 and 2 |
| Sync ambition | Two-way Drive from the start, backup first then two-way, or iCloud sync with Drive backup | Two-way Drive with keep-both conflicts, built in phase 2 |
| Mac app | Native app for reading, typing and light annotation, or the iPad app running on Mac with the full canvas | Native |
| Minimum system | iPadOS 27 and macOS 27, or support older versions | Require 27; free handwriting search and stable stroke IDs depend on it |
| Document types | Pages only, or pages plus infinite canvas | Pages only until phase 3 |
| Extras | Audio replay, layers, AI, math | Audio replay first; it is cheap and widely loved |

## How the research was checked

Four research passes were run on DeepSeek and reviewed afterwards. Twenty-four source pages were then fetched again and checked for the specific claims they support, including Google's quota, scope and testing-mode pages, Apple's documentation for the new 2026 APIs, both apps' pricing and release notes, and the two App Store review feeds, where all ten reviewer names sampled were present; all matched. The review corrected four points. Goodnotes is now on version 7, not 6 ([App Store lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1444383602&country=us)). PencilKit has eight pen styles, not three. On-device speech transcription is a public API. PDF link handling exists in Apple's framework.

Several things remain unverified: the vote counts on Goodnotes' request board, the on-device AI context size, Goodnotes' description of its own engine, how Google Drive behaves through the Files app, the iCloud, Dropbox and OneDrive comparisons, and every effort estimate. OneNote and Noteful were not researched because their sites timed out. No published timeline or post-mortem from a solo developer of an ink app was found, so the phase ordering reflects technical dependency and not measured effort.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the project more than its feature list. Before it, the hard part looked like the pen: latency, palm rejection, recognition. In 2026 the system supplies those, and the hard part has moved to the two things incumbents are failing at in public, which are keeping notes safe and keeping them yours. An app whose notebooks are readable files in your own Drive, with no account of its own and no paywall that can lock a notebook, answers the loudest complaints in the category by design.

That also sets the order of work. The file format and the conflict rules deserve the first design effort, before any toolbar, because they decide whether sync, audio replay and layers are later additions or rewrites. A useful first prototype is therefore not a drawing screen but two devices editing the same page offline and reconciling through Drive.
